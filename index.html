What is the mistake log?

It is a running list of every question you got wrong, skipped, or guessed right by luck. You add to it for 30-45 minutes each night, and after every mock as part of the analysis. It turns mistakes into a to-do list, so you stop losing marks to the same error twice.

What to write for each question:

  • Where it came from (paper or set, question number)
  • Section and topic
  • Error type: concept gap, calculation, ran out of time, bad question selection, or silly slip
  • The correct approach in one line
  • A redo date

How to use it: redo every entry after 3 days and again after 10. A topic that shows up in the log twice or more becomes your next priority. Keep it in a notebook or a spreadsheet. The tick in the tracker only means you did the update.

Approximate proportions based on the papers I know best (strongest from 2015 onwards). Treat them as patterns, not exact counts.

  • 2005-2014: different format (two sections), so only the topic patterns are useful.
  • 2015-2019: about 100 questions, 3 sections of 60 minutes each, generally easier.

Skip advanced trigonometry, matrices and the binomial theorem. They are rare or absent.

DILR (about 22 questions, 4-5 sets)

  • Must-Do: arrangements (linear, circular, matrix), selection/grouping with conditions, tournaments/scheduling, tables and caselets.
  • Should-Do: games/puzzles, networks/routes, Venn-based sets.
  • Skip as a first pick in the exam: very calculation-heavy multi-graph sets.

VARC (about 24 questions)

  • Must-Do: RCs (about 16-18 questions from 4 passages) and para jumbles (TITA). Skip the long jumbles in the exam.
  • Should-Do: para summary and odd-one-out.
  • Skip: grammar rules and vocabulary lists. They have not been tested in years.

Best marks per hour for a beginner: QA arithmetic + algebra + number system first, then daily RC practice. DILR has the highest variance. It needs volume (about 80 sets solved) and the habit of picking the right sets.

Mock strategy and score targets

How many? You cannot fit 50. This plan gives 16 full mocks plus 12-15 sectionals, roughly 25 tests' worth of practice. A mock without analysis is wasted, so cut mocks before you cut analysis.

When: a diagnostic on Day 7, then regularly from Day 21. Take mocks at your real slot time once you know it. Use past CAT slot papers (2021-2025) for some Phase 3 mocks.

The 3-hour analysis

  • 40 min on attempt quality: what you attempted, what you should have skipped, where the time went.
  • 40 min on VARC: re-read each wrong RC and note why the right option wins.
  • 50 min on DILR: re-solve every unsolved set untimed. This is where most learning happens.
  • 40 min on QA: redo each wrong question and classify the error.
  • 10 min to update the mistake log.

Videos: short or long?
  • Phase 1: long videos for QA concepts, at 1.25-1.5x speed, one topic at a time. Do questions on that topic the same day. Keep videos under 40% of QA time.
  • After Phase 1: short videos only to fix a specific doubt or learn a shortcut.
  • VARC and DILR: no concept binges. One or two strategy sessions, then practise.
  • PYQs: your main material. Topic-wise for QA after each concept, PYQ sets for DILR from Day 8, PYQ RCs daily from Day 1. Treat CAT 2017-2025 slot papers as full mocks later.
  • One source per section. Do not hop between channels. Pick one paid mock series alongside the free past papers.
Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Do RCs every day from Day 1, even when you feel behind.
  • Time everything from Day 10 onwards.
  • Practise skipping: leaving a bad question or set is a skill.
  • Keep the nightly mistake-log session, even on light days.
  • Protect sleep, especially in the last 10 days.

Don't

  • Do not read grammar or vocabulary books.
  • Do not start new topics after about Day 40.
  • Do not chase every shortcut trick.
  • Do not compare your mock percentile to others' in the first three weeks.
  • Do not take a mock and skip the analysis.
  • Do not change strategy in the last 3 days.
Exam-day strategy
  • VARC (40 min): skim all 4 passages for 1-2 minutes, then start with the easiest. About 8 minutes per RC, and the VA questions (PJ, PS, OOO) in about 10 minutes. Aim for 16-18 attempts and skip dense passages.
  • DILR (40 min): spend the first 4-5 minutes scanning all sets and pick the 2 easiest. Solve them fully (about 15 minutes each), then try a third. If you are stuck for more than 5-6 minutes with no progress, switch. Aim for 10-12 questions.
  • QA (40 min): first pass over all 22 questions in about 20 minutes, taking the easy and TITA ones. Second pass on the medium ones, and leave 4-5 hard ones. Aim for 14-16 attempts.

You cannot carry time across sections, so use each 40 minutes fully.

If your mocks plateau
  • Stuck below ~85 percentile: concept gaps. Go back to topic-wise PYQs.
  • High attempts, low accuracy: you are rushing or guessing. Slow down and cut your weakest 20% of attempts.
  • Low attempts, high accuracy: you are too cautious. Run timed sets with a hard 2-minute limit per question.
  • DILR is stuck: usually set selection, not skill. Run the 5-sets-in-5-minutes drill daily.
  • VARC is stuck: classify wrong RC answers as inference, tone or detail errors and drill that type.
  • No change after 3 mocks: stop mocks for 2 days and do only error repair.

Is 99.5+ realistic?

It is a long shot. This is my judgement, not a measured statistic. Starting from near zero in all three sections with about 45 effective study days, a 95-98 percentile is the realistic zone if you follow the plan, 99+ is possible if QA and DILR click, and 99.5+ needs most things to go right. A CS background should help with QA and DILR, while VARC from scratch will be the slowest to move. The most common route to 99.5+ is QA at 95+, DILR at 90+, and steady RC accuracy.
